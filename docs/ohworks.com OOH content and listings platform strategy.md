# oohworks.co: OOH content and listings platform strategy

Oct 9, 2026&#32;

## Executive summary

oohworks.co can own a lane no one holds today: a neutral, verified, Google-indexed page for every billboard and screen, paired with independent industry news, with every enquiry going straight to the media owner. OOH is a $54.2bn market that grew 15% in 2025, yet most of it is still found and booked offline.

**The opportunity**

- **Supply is invisible.** Independent owners leave 30–40% of their faces unsold. Only 8.4% of digital OOH trades programmatically, and static boards have no online sales route at all.
- **Buyers can't find or trust inventory.** Small businesses hit contact forms, agencies wait about a week for proposals, and Indian buyers face fake proof photos and illegal sites.
- **Search is won by thin pages.** AdQuick, Blip and BillboardsIn stop at city level. The Media Ant's per-hoarding pages in India lack real photos, size, map, audience and owner name.
- **No one combines news with live listings.** Trade titles carry no inventory, and marketplaces publish no journalism.

**Recommended positioning:** "The open map of out-of-home, where the owner is always the seller." Buyers use it free. Owners list free, set their own price and keep their clients. OOHWORKS never sells media, and says so in a published charter.

**The moves that matter most**

1. **One verified page per physical face,** with a strict content bar before Google sees it, and city hubs published only when they hold real inventory.
2. **Revenue from owners' tools and visibility,** never media margin: Pro and Network subscriptions, labelled featured placement, classifieds and sponsorship.
3. **Launch in Indian metros** with association partners, free assisted onboarding and a founding-owner programme, then the UAE and Saudi Arabia.
4. **Two content tracks wired into the listings:** a trade newsroom and buyer cost guides, plus a quarterly price index the trade press will re-report.

**The biggest risk** is drifting into agency work, as India's best-known listings marketplaces, The Media Ant and GoHoardings, have done. Close behind are stale listings and Google treating the page set as thin.

## The OOH content and blogging landscape

No global OOH publication exists. Each region has one or two small specialist titles, and none of them shows live inventory or prices. Buyer searches such as "how much does a billboard cost" are won instead by marketplaces running thin, often stale template pages. OOHWORKS can be the first platform to combine real trade journalism with live, priced listings.

### What works in this niche

- **Newsletters.** Billboard Insider's 59% open rate is about double the norm. DailyDOOH sends five issues a week.
- **Awards and events are the real business.** Examples are OAAA's OBIE Awards (83rd edition), the e4m Neons (14th edition) and Media4Growth's OOH Advertising Awards.
- **Original data gets re-reported everywhere.** WOO's first SSP-based programmatic DOOH study (September 2026, $1.34bn) was picked up by MediaPost, The Media Leader and Bizcommunity within days ([Billboard Insider](https://billboardinsider.com/global-programmatic-dooh-market-was-1-3-billion-in-2025/)). It counts differently from the $2.1bn survey figure above.
- **Creative showcases travel.** 3D anamorphic billboards and iconic sites get shared well beyond the trade.
- **Cost questions dominate buyer search.** Six of the top ten OOH searches were about cost in a 2023 analysis by 75Media ([Billboard Insider](https://billboardinsider.com/lack-of-knowledge-still-a-barrier-for-ooh/)).
- **Podcasts are small.** Billboard Insider's averages about 370 downloads an episode.

### Who wins buyer search today

Marketplaces win buyer queries with programmatic page sets, not publishers. AdQuick runs `/billboard-cost/{city-state}` pages down to small towns plus a long-tail Answers library ([example](https://www.adquick.com/billboard-cost/redwood-city-ca)). Blip runs `/billboard-locations/{state}/{city}/`. In India, [The Media Ant](https://www.themediaant.com/outdoor?page=0) and SmartAds rank with per-hoarding and city pages.

Quality is weak across the board. AdQuick's Redwood City page has a template error in its meta text. The Media Ant's top Mumbai guide was last updated in 2019. Spam pages on hijacked domains rank for "billboard cost 2026". AdQuick has already added "summarise with ChatGPT or Claude" buttons, so the leaders are optimising for AI answers.

### Gaps OOHWORKS can own

1. **Dated, sourced prices** by city and format, especially outside the US.
2. **Emerging markets in local terms**: "hoarding" in India, "vía pública" and "espectacular" in Spanish, "mídia exterior" in Brazil.
3. **Practical help for small media owners**: permits, site leases, the economics of converting a board to digital, and selling through programmatic exchanges.
4. **Independent analysis** above the press-release feed.
5. **Community.** Today it is tiny: r/DigitalOOH has 217 members, and Billboard Insider's Zoom networking group launched with 100.
6. **Site and portfolio sales.** Billboard Insider's for-sale classifieds show demand for buying and selling whole boards, not just renting them.

The nearest models from other B2B markets are Houzz (inspiration content, a professional directory with $55–$999 monthly tiers, and a 15% marketplace commission), G2 and Capterra (category, comparison and alternatives pages fed by reviews), and Digiday (newsletter first, then events and awards).

### What this means for OOHWORKS

- **Per-billboard pages are an open lane.** AdQuick, Blip, BillboardsIn and Blindspot stop at city, ZIP or format level. Unit detail sits behind logins or crawler-blocked maps; Lamar's 360,000-display finder blocks search engines.
- **Free listing is not a differentiator.** DOmedia's base tier is 0%, Blip charges no upfront fees and The Media Ant lists for free. OOHWORKS must win on demand, page quality and owner tools.
- **Agency drift is the main business risk.** The Media Ant (free listings in 2015) and GoHoardings (2016) now call themselves agencies; earlier entrants EatAds (2013) and MeraHoardings (2015) went quiet. OOHWORKS needs non-agency revenue from launch.
- **Big owners are picking sides.** Outfront backs AdQuick, JCDecaux owns VIOOH and Displayce, and T-Mobile agreed to buy Vistar for $600M. Independents may prefer a neutral venue.
- **Capital went to programmatic, not listings.** T-Mobile agreed to buy Vistar for $600M and Perion bought Hivestack for $100M; AdQuick raised about $15M in total. A directory is defensible only through owner workflow lock-in and real demand.
- **Trust is weak.** AdQuick quotes 900, 1,700+ and 423 media owners on different pages. Blip scores 2.1 out of 5 on G2, with complaints about no proof that ads played and no board addresses ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/blip-billboards/reviews)).
- **The Gulf has no listings site.** A few large owners (Al-Arabia, BackLite, Pikasso) digitise through global exchanges. OutdoorAdvertisingUAE.com publishes road and district pages but no prices.

## User behaviour

Buying OOH is still slow, relationship-led and opaque on every side. Agencies send requests to dozens of vendors and wait about a week, small businesses look for a price and find a contact form, and independent owners leave 30–40% of their inventory unsold. OOHWORKS wins if it makes the first step fast: finding comparable, trustworthy site data and reaching the right owner.

| Audience | How they act today | What frustrates them | What they need from OOHWORKS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agencies and OOH specialists | Send RFPs to many vendors; proposals take about a week; keep a private list of independents | Slow replies, stale availability, inconsistent site data | Standard site data, fast multi-owner availability requests, exportable plans, no contact with their clients |
| Digital and programmatic planners new to OOH | Buy DOOH through private deals on exchanges; money comes from other digital budgets | No digital-grade data, hard to compare with online channels | Audience figures with a named source, specs, a "programmatic via" flag, education content |
| Small businesses and first-time advertisers | Search "how much does a billboard cost", find contact forms, give up | Hidden prices, slow quotes, fear it is unaffordable | A "from" price, photos, an exact location and a one-step enquiry |
| Independent media owners | Sell by phone, email and WhatsApp; track bookings in spreadsheets | Unsold faces, lost leads, late payment | Free qualified leads, an online storefront, an availability calendar |
| Large media owners | Run their own sales teams and exchange connections | Commoditisation, platforms tied to rivals | Brand visibility, neutral demand, control of what is shown |

### Buyers

- **Lead times are short enough to need live availability.** AdQuick bookings land a median 25 days before launch: 36 days for static and 14 for digital ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/billboard-costs/how-long-is-the-minimum-lead-time-for-dooh-formats-and-creative)).
- **Agencies ask for short, accurate site sheets.** They want location, size, lighting, street-level photos (close-ups are not helpful), audience and price. Fast responses were their top request ([OAAA](https://oaaa.org/blog-posts/agencyinsights/), 2018).
- **Programmatic is still a minority.** It is about 30% of US DOOH spend ([OAAA](https://oaaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025-OOH-Ad-Spend-Performance-Facts-and-Figures-FINAL.pdf)) and about 2% in the Gulf ([Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/spent-13-years-buying-ooh-then-i-crossed-the-floor-and-sold-it-heres-what-nobody-tells-you/)). Two-thirds of programmatic spend runs through custom private deals ([Billboard Insider](https://billboardinsider.com/broadsign-issues-2026-programmatic-report/)).
- **New buyers come from digital teams.** In VIOOH's 2026 survey, 95% of buyers raising programmatic DOOH spend move money from other digital channels ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/pdooh-investment-set-to-surge-44-as-programmatic-takes-over-half-of-campaigns/)). They expect digital-grade data and speed.
- **Measurement is the top objection.** It is the main barrier for 58% of UK planners ([MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/azerion-study-the-state-of-uk-dooh/)); ROI tops the list in Australia at 42%.
- **Relationships still decide premium sites.** A former Gulf buyer says the best sites go to the agency with the deepest relationship.

### Small businesses

- **Hidden prices lose them.** One UK business was quoted £650 for a Leeds 48-sheet that an agency publishes at £325 ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/industry-insight/why-does-out-of-home-still-hide-its-prices)).
- **Published prices pull demand, including from AI assistants.** That agency reports 266% more enquiries from AI-tool referrals after publishing machine-readable prices (self-reported).
- **Entry points are lower than people think.** Blip starts at $20 a day; AdQuick's median static billboard campaign is $10,900.

### Media owners

- **Inventory goes unsold.** The US benchmark is 80% occupancy, but independents often run 60–70%, and a new digital board takes six months or more to fill ([Billboard Insider](https://billboardinsider.com/readers-say-a-digital-takes-xx-mos-to-rent-up-and-xx-is-full-occupancy/)).
- **Payment is slow.** US agencies take 90–120 days. Indian owners describe delays of up to a year and being blacklisted for chasing payment (2021, [Media4Growth](https://www.media4growth.com/ooh-solutions/delayed-payments-margin-squeeze-burden-innovation-fabrication-mounting-business-5709)).
- **Their fears are specific.** Blip heard "you'll cannibalise our business", "you'll devalue our rates" and "you'll steal our customers" from 400+ owners. It won them by bringing extra demand, sending big or long deals back to the owner's sales team, and letting them start with one or two slots ([Blip](https://www.blipbillboards.com/blog/the-maps-framework-for-shared-inventory)).
- **Indian owners want their own brand.** 82% of 33 owner heads surveyed said owners should invest in their brand to avoid commoditisation (2023, [Media4Growth](https://www.media4growth.com/ooh-news/voice-of-indian-ooh-industry-6985)).

### Trust

- **Indian buyers face real fraud.** invidis lists inflated traffic data, one structure sold to several clients, swapped locations, and fake or AI-edited proof-of-posting photos ([invidis](https://invidis.com/news/2026/08/indias-ooh-trust-deficit-five-challenges-holding-back-the-market/), August 2026).
- **Illegal sites are widespread.** About 4,000 of 6,000 Bengaluru hoardings were illegal in a 2016 count ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/advertising/bbmp-chief-commissioner-directs-officials-to-take-strict-action-against-unauthorised-ads-53278.htm)). Mumbai published its list of approved sites in January 2025 after court rebukes.
- **Liability is moving to buyers.** IOAA warns that booking a site missing from its planned survey puts liability on the owner and the client ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/advertising/ooh-industry-to-clock-rs-5500-crore-adex-in-2025-praveen-vadhera-ceo-of-ioaa-52032.htm)).

Verification, not volume, is therefore the trust feature that matters: a checked owner, a permit reference, geotagged photos and a "last verified" date on every listing.

Position OOHWORKS as the open, verified map of the world's OOH inventory, where the media owner is always the seller. Owners list free, keep their price and their client, and receive enquiries directly. Agencies and brands use OOHWORKS free to find, check and request sites. OOHWORKS earns from owner tools and visibility, never from a media margin.

### What OOHWORKS promises, and never does

These five rules answer the fears owners and agencies raised in the research. Publish them on the site as a charter.

1. **We never sell media or take a media margin.** That is the line India's best-known marketplaces crossed.
2. **The owner sets the price** and chooses how it shows: exact rate, "from" price or on request.
3. **The owner owns the client.** Enquiries go straight to the owner; we never resell or reroute them.
4. **We never contact a buyer's clients** or share an agency's plans with anyone.
5. **Every number shows its source,** and paid placements are always labelled.

## Product blueprint 1: the editorial and blogging platform

Run two content tracks on one platform and wire both into live listings. The trade track (news, data, analysis) builds authority and community among owners and agencies. The buyer track (cost guides, explainers, showcases) wins search traffic and turns readers into enquiries.

### Content pillars

| Pillar | Main reader | Formats | Example | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| News and analysis | Owners, agencies | News, contracts, people moves, regulation, weekly analysis | "What Bengaluru's draft hoarding auction rules mean for owners" | Daily news, one analysis a week |
| OOHWORKS data | Trade, press, buyers | Price index by city and format, market reports built from listing data | "India hoarding price index, Q1 2027" | Quarterly |
| Buyer guides | Brands, small businesses, digital planners | City and format cost guides, "what is DOOH", format comparisons, how to measure a first campaign | "Billboard advertising cost in Bengaluru" | Evergreen, refreshed quarterly from live data |
| Creative showcase | Everyone | Billboard of the week, 3D anamorphic roundups, case studies linked to the sites used | "10 best Gulf DOOH campaigns of 2026" | Weekly |
| Owner academy | Media owners | Permits by city, site leases, pricing a board, converting to digital, joining programmatic exchanges | "How to get a hoarding licence in Hyderabad" | Two guides a month |
| Community | Everyone | Q&A, verified profiles, jobs, events calendar, sites-for-sale classifieds | "Ask an owner" threads | Ongoing |

### How content connects to listings

- **Every guide shows live inventory.** A Bengaluru cost guide embeds Bengaluru listings with their "from" prices and an enquiry button.
- **Case studies link to sites.** A campaign write-up links to each billboard it used, and each listing shows the campaigns that ran on it.
- **One shared tag system.** Location, format, owner, brand and topic tags join articles, listings and profiles, so every page links sideways to related ones.
- **Data flows one way.** Listing data feeds the price index and the cost guides automatically; editors add context, not numbers.

### Authors and trust

- **In-house editors per launch market** write news and analysis. Each has an author page with credentials.
- **A verified contributor programme** gives industry experts bylined columns, which strengthens expertise signals for search and AI answers.
- **Owner updates** (new site launches, network news) sit in a separate, clearly labelled stream.
- **Sponsored content is always labelled** and kept out of news.

### Editions and language

- Country editions under subfolders: `/in/`, `/ae/`, `/sa/`, `/us/`, `/uk/`.
- Use local terms in titles and copy: "hoarding" in India, "vía pública" and "espectacular" in Spanish, "mídia exterior" in Brazil.
- Publish in Hindi, Arabic, Spanish or Portuguese only where people search in that language. Have people write or edit it; bulk machine translation risks Google's scaled-content penalties (section on location SEO).

### Newsletters

- **A daily trade briefing** per region. Niche OOH newsletters reach 59% open rates.
- **A weekly buyer digest:** "New and available sites in your city this week", personalised by followed cities and formats.

### Reader experience

- Fast server-rendered pages, a table of contents on long guides, reading time and dark mode.
- Follow a city, format, owner or topic, and get alerts for new articles and new sites.
- Interactive price charts and maps inside articles.
- Every fact carries its source and date, which helps both readers and AI answer engines cite OOHWORKS.

### CMS and workflow

Use a headless CMS (Payload, Sanity or Strapi) with structured content types: Article, Guide, Market report, Case study, Event, Job, Person and Company. The workflow is draft, edit, fact-check, publish, with scheduling and version history. Structured data, sitemaps and internal links are generated from the content model, not added by hand.

### Launch content

1. Cost guides for each launch city and main format, each backed by at least 20 real listings.
2. A glossary of OOH terms. Its ranking value in OOH is untested, so measure it.
3. Twenty owner academy guides on permits, pricing and listing well.
4. From launch: ten news pieces, one analysis and one showcase roundup a week.
5. The first quarterly price index once each city has enough priced listings.

## Product blueprint 2: the inventory marketplace

Build the marketplace around one page per physical advertising face, owned by a verified media owner, with every enquiry routed straight to that owner. OOHWORKS stays out of the money flow. It supplies the listing, the demand and the tools around the enquiry.

### Media owner onboarding

1. **Sign up and verify the company** with registration documents (GSTIN in India, a trade licence in the UAE) and a phone or WhatsApp code.
2. **Import inventory** from an Excel template, bulk photo upload or API. Offer free assisted onboarding: the OOHWORKS team turns an owner's PDF media kit or spreadsheet into listings. Inventory-loading effort is a documented reason owners abandon marketplaces.
3. **Pin each face on a map** and check it against Street View. The system flags likely duplicates of faces already listed.
4. **Add photos** of the face by day, by night and from the approach road. Photos taken in the OOHWORKS app carry GPS and time stamps and earn a verified-photo badge.
5. **Set commercial terms:** price display (exact, "from" or on request), minimum period, printing and mounting costs, and categories accepted.
6. **Set availability** in a simple calendar, or sync it from the owner's system.
7. **Go live.** Each listing gets a quality score. Listings below the indexing bar stay visible to signed-in buyers but hidden from Google until enriched.

### Verification badges

| Badge | What OOHWORKS checks |
| --- | --- |
| Verified owner | Company documents and contact details |
| Verified location | Coordinates match Street View or a GPS-stamped site photo |
| Permit on file | Permit or licence number, issuing authority and expiry date |
| Fresh | Availability or photos updated within the last 30 days |
| Responsive | Owner's median reply time is under 24 hours |

### Listing data model

Align fields to industry standards from day one so listings can feed agency tools and programmatic exchanges later.

| Group | Key fields | Standard followed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Permanent unit ID; structure > face > slot; external IDs (Geopath, Route/SPACE, RoadStar, OpenRTB screen ID) | Geopath inventory hierarchy |
| Classification | Venue type ID and taxonomy version; environment (roadside, transit, retail, indoor); local format name (hoarding, unipole, 48-sheet, bulletin); static or digital | [OpenOOH venue taxonomy v1.2](https://github.com/openooh/venue-taxonomy/blob/main/specification-1.2.md) |
| Location | Latitude and longitude to 5+ decimals; facing in degrees; road; landmark; locality, city, region, country; venue name for indoor units | Google's location precision guidance |
| Physical | Width and height stored in millimetres, shown in feet or metres by locale; illumination type and hours | [Geopath taxonomy](https://geopath.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GEOP/pages/2323448476/Geopath+Unified+Inventory+Taxonomy) |
| Digital | Resolution, spot length, loop length, slots, motion and audio, operating hours, programmatic exchanges | [OpenRTB 2.6](https://iabtechlab.com/standards/openrtb/) and AdCOM |
| Audience | Metric, period, demographic base, source, method and date; never shown without a source | OpenRTB impression multiplier |
| Commercial | Rate per period and currency, price display mode, minimum period, production costs, availability calendar, sellers and their role | — |
| Proof | Dated day, night and approach photos; verification status and date; permit number and authority | — |

### Buyer experience

- **Search** by map or list, by place ("near Silk Board junction"), format, size, lighting, price band, dates, audience and programmatic availability. Add natural-language search: "20×10 hoardings on Outer Ring Road under ₹1 lakh a month in November".
- **Listing page:** photos, static map, specs, audience with its source, price, availability, an owner card with badges and reply time, campaigns that ran on the site, and similar sites nearby.
- **Plans:** save sites into a plan, share it by link, and export a standard site sheet as PDF, Excel or slides. Agencies can white-label exports.
- **Enquiries:** one form for one site or a whole plan. OOHWORKS splits a multi-owner plan into one request per owner, with dates, budget, brand category and creative status.
- **Agency workspace:** team seats, private client folders, enquiry history, and owner replies side by side.

OOHWORKS's only job in the flow is to check the buyer and route the request. Contract, invoice and payment stay between buyer and owner, and the owner's calendar updates when the site is marked booked.

### Media owner dashboard

- **Leads inbox** across web, email and WhatsApp, with reply templates and quotes. WhatsApp matters: Indian, Gulf and African owners already sell this way.
- **Analytics per site:** page views, search appearances, shortlists, enquiries and replies.
- **Availability and occupancy** calendar.
- **Storefront page** at `oohworks.co/owners/{name}`, a free mini-site for owners with no web presence.
- **Proof of posting:** upload geotagged photos that the buyer can see.
- **Team seats** for sales staff.

### Trust and safety

- **One page per face.** When an owner and a reseller list the same face, it stays one page with several offers; the verified owner's offer shows first.
- **Stale listings are flagged.** No update in 30 days marks availability as unconfirmed.
- **Image checks** catch stock photos and images reused across listings.
- **Buyer checks** (work email or phone code) protect   owners from spam enquiries.
- **Report a listing** on every page, reviewed within a set time.

### Bridges to the rest of the industry

- **Programmatic:** digital listings show "available programmatically via" a named exchange (Lemma, Moving Walls, Place Exchange, VIOOH), so OOHWORKS complements rather than competes.
- **Owner systems:** import and sync from Broadsign, Billboard Planet and similar tools.
- **AI assistants:** a public listings API, and later an MCP server, so ChatGPT and Claude can quote OOHWORKS inventory. OneScreen already exposes about 1M US listings this way.

## Location SEO architecture

Every billboard can be findable on Google, but only if each page is the real destination for a real site, carrying facts no other page has. Google's spam policy (updated 28 August 2026) names city pages that funnel users to one page as doorway abuse, and bulk-generated or machine-translated pages as scaled content abuse ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). The city hubs, not the billboard pages, carry the most risk, so publish a hub only when it has real inventory.

### URL structure and what gets indexed

Use one hostname with country subfolders. Google treats each hostname as a separate site for crawling, so subdomains would split the crawl budget ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/large-site-managing-crawl-budget)).

| Page type | URL pattern | Index it when | Structured data |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Billboard (unit) | `/in/bengaluru/koramangala/hoarding-80-feet-road-ow8k3x2/` | It meets the content bar below | Product or Place, Offer (lease), coordinates, images, breadcrumbs |
| Locality hub | `/in/bengaluru/koramangala/` | It has at least 10 indexable units (starting threshold to test) | Breadcrumbs, item list |
| City or format hub | `/in/bengaluru/` · `/in/bengaluru/hoardings/` | It has at least 25 indexable units (starting threshold to test) | Breadcrumbs, item list |
| Owner storefront | `/owners/{owner-name}/` | Owner is verified with one or more indexable units | Organization or LocalBusiness |
| Guide or article | `/in/guides/billboard-advertising-cost-bengaluru/` | Always | Article with author |
| Filtered or sorted views | `?size=…&sort=…` | Never: blocked in robots.txt; empty combinations return 404 | — |

- **Put a permanent ID at the end of every unit URL.** If the slug changes, redirect the old URL permanently. Never put price, availability or seller in a URL.
- **Add language versions only where people search in that language,** for example `/ae/ar/` or `/in/hi/`, linked with hreflang. Never copy Indian billboards into a "US English" version.
- **Never redirect visitors automatically by IP.** Show a country banner instead.

### The content bar for a billboard page

A unit page goes into the sitemap only when it has all of these. Below the bar it stays `noindex` until the owner enriches it.

- [ ] Verified coordinates to 5+ decimals, and facing direction
- [ ] At least one real, dated photo of the face; ideally day, night and approach
- [ ] Format, size and lighting, plus screen specs if digital
- [ ] A named, verified media owner
- [ ] A price or "price on request", and availability
- [ ] Audience with its source, or no audience figure at all
- [ ] A static map and nearby landmarks drawn from map data
- [ ] A short factual summary built from the site's data, not generic filler

### When inventory changes

- **Booked:** keep the page live, show "booked until" with the next available date, and list similar nearby sites.
- **Dismantled:** return 410, or redirect only to a true replacement at the same spot. Mass redirects to the city hub risk being read as soft 404s.
- **Duplicate listings:** one page per physical face, with several sellers' offers on it.

### Getting hundreds of thousands of pages indexed

- **Sitemaps** split by country, city and page type, each under 50,000 URLs, grouped in sitemap index files ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/large-sitemaps)).
- **Update `lastmod` only on real changes** (photos, specs, price, availability). Google uses it only when it is consistently accurate.
- **IndexNow** pushes changes to Bing, Yandex, Naver and Seznam, up to 10,000 URLs a request. Google does not take part.
- **Do not use Google's Indexing API.** It is only for job postings and livestreams, and misuse can be revoked.
- **Internal links do the work:** homepage "new sites" modules, hubs to units, units to nearby units, guides to units.
- **Launch in stages.** Release the best-data markets first rather than 500,000 thin pages at once. Google says crawling takes a few days to a few weeks.
- **Prune monthly.** Enrich or `noindex` pages with no search impressions after a set period.

### Structured data

- **No billboard type exists in schema.org.** Use `Product` or `Place` with an `Offer` marked as a lease, a price per week or month, coordinates, and `additionalProperty` for size, lighting and specs.
- **Mark up a price only when the page shows one.** Google's docs don't say whether rented ad space qualifies for product rich results.
- **FAQ rich results ended on 7 May 2026.** Keep FAQs as visible text only.
- **No Google Business Profile per billboard.** Billboards have no staff or opening hours, and rental properties are ineligible. Owners' offices can have profiles that link to their OOHWORKS storefront.

### Rendering and speed

- Server-render every listing and hub page (Next.js incremental static regeneration, or Astro), returning real status codes.
- Show a static map image first and load the interactive map only on click.
- Serve photos as responsive AVIF or WebP with fixed dimensions. Target Google's Core Web Vitals: main image or text visible within 2.5 s, taps answered within 200 ms, and a layout shift score under 0.1.
- One background job per listing change: regenerate the page, update the sitemap date and ping IndexNow.

### AI search

- Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode need nothing beyond normal indexing, and Google Search does not use `llms.txt` ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).
- Allow ChatGPT's and Perplexity's search crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot). Decide separately whether to allow AI training crawlers.
- Open every hub with a dated fact from OOHWORKS data, such as "Bengaluru has N listed hoardings; median 20×10 ft rate ₹X a month (OOHWORKS, Oct 2026)". Original figures like this are what answer engines quote.

## Business model and go-to-market

Keep buyers free forever and charge media owners for visibility and tools, never for the media itself. Launch in India, starting with Bengaluru and the large metros, then the Gulf, then the US, UK, Africa and Latin America. Win supply city by city before spending on demand.

### Revenue streams

| Stream | Who pays | What they get | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free tier | Nobody | Unlimited listings, storefront, enquiries, basic analytics | Launch |
| Owner Pro | Independent owners | Full analytics, WhatsApp leads inbox, availability sync, verified-photo app, priority support | Phase 2 |
| Owner Network | Large owners | Bulk import and API, team seats, custom storefront domain, account manager | Phase 2 |
| Featured placement | Owners | Labelled "Featured" slots on hubs and search results, capped per page | Phase 2 |
| Classifieds | Owners, employers | Sites and structures for sale, jobs; a fixed fee per listing (Billboard Insider charges $175 for 60 days) | Phase 2 |
| Sponsorship | Ad-tech firms, screen makers, printers | Labelled newsletter, report and guide sponsorships | Phase 1 onward |
| Data | Owners, investors, agencies | Quarterly price index reports, market data API | Phase 3 |
| Events and awards | The industry | Awards and a conference once the audience exists | Phase 3 onward |

Set Pro and Network prices after a pilot with 20–30 owners. Anchor them to the value of one extra booking a year, not to rival SaaS price lists.

**What to avoid:** a commission on bookings. It puts OOHWORKS in the money flow, reawakens agency fears and needs payment handling. Revisit only as an optional, flat-fee payment-protection service if owners ask for it; slow payment (90–120 days, up to a year in India) is a real pain.

### Winning supply

1. **Start where owners need demand most.** Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Pune and Chennai first, then Tier-2 cities. There, occupancy is lower, specialist agencies are weaker and owners already sell direct.
2. **Partner with associations.** Work with IOAA and state owner associations for legitimacy and supply, as Nigeria's OAAN did with its own marketplace.
3. **Offer assisted onboarding:** "send us your media kit, we'll list it within 24 hours".
4. **Run a founding-owner programme.** The first 100 verified owners get Pro free for a year and a permanent founding badge.
5. **Show up where owners gather:** Media4Growth's OAC convention (July), the e4m OOH conference (April) and regional owner meetings.
6. **Watch Bengaluru's rules.** The draft 2025 rules would auction road stretches to licensed bidders, which could concentrate supply in fewer, larger owners. Confirm their final status before planning Bengaluru supply targets.

### Creating demand

- **Cost guides and city hubs** for each launch city, live as soon as each city clears the inventory threshold.
- **Search ads** on "hoarding advertising cost {city}" queries to seed early enquiries while organic rankings build.
- **A free agency workspace,** demonstrated to OOH specialist agencies with the neutrality charter up front.
- **The newsletter** from day one; a quarterly price index from the first quarter with enough priced listings.
- **Owner-driven traffic:** every owner storefront link an owner shares brings buyers to OOHWORKS.

### Market sequence

| Wave | Markets | Why | Entry angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | India metros, then Tier-2 | Home market, fragmented supply, weak rival pages, trust problems to solve | Verified, owner-branded listings and permit data |
| 2 | UAE, Saudi Arabia | Most digital markets in the world, few owners, no listings site | International discovery for a handful of large owners; Arabic and English |
| 3 | US, UK | Largest spend, but DOmedia and AdQuick are present | Independents, per-billboard pages and editorial |
| 4 | Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa; Brazil, Mexico | Mostly static, owners pushing for online discovery | Association partnerships; local-language guides |

## Risks and open questions

The biggest risks are commercial, not technical: earning money without agency margin, keeping listings fresh, and avoiding Google treating the page set as thin.

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drift into agency work | The Media Ant and GoHoardings both moved from listings to agency margin | Publish the charter; build revenue on owner subscriptions from Phase 2 |
| Owners let listings go stale | Stale availability breaks buyer trust and weakens pages | "Fresh" badge, 30-day staleness flag, one-tap WhatsApp availability check, sync with owner systems |
| Owners refuse to show prices | They fear rate erosion | Three price display modes; reward transparent owners with a badge, never force a price |
| Google treats pages as thin or doorways | A penalty can hit the whole domain | Content bar, hub thresholds, staged launch, monthly pruning, no bulk machine translation |
| Large owners stay away | Outfront, JCDecaux and T-Mobile already back rival platforms | Neutral charter, Network tier with API; start with independents |
| Fake or illegal listings | Legal liability and lost trust | Verification badges, permit field, photo checks, report-a-listing |
| Sudden regulation | Bengaluru auctions and Mumbai's approval freeze reshaped supply quickly | Track regulation in the newsroom; keep permit data per site |
| A rival adds per-billboard SEO pages | The Media Ant or Moving Walls could close the lane | Move fast on data depth, owner relationships and editorial |
| Investors doubt directories | Exits went to programmatic platforms, not listings | Show owner subscription revenue and enquiry volume early |
